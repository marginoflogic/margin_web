We love to trade breakouts. We see a massive surge of buying volume hitting a key resistance level, and our immediate instinct is to jump in. We assume that if everyone is buying, the price has no choice but to go up. But then the market completely freezes. Despite the heavy buying pressure, the price refuses to move a single pip higher, and shortly after, it aggressively reverses, stopping us all out. We are left confused, thinking the system is broken.

The reality is that we just ran into an institutional absorption protocol, commonly executed via iceberg orders. Market makers and large funds cannot simply execute a massive position all at once, or they would completely destroy their own execution price. Instead, they slice their massive orders into tiny, invisible pieces. When we aggressively buy that breakout, we are actually just feeding our liquidity directly into their massive hidden sell order.

They are quietly absorbing all of our buying pressure without revealing their true size on the order book. Once our collective buying power is completely exhausted, the absorption stops. The trap is set, and the price immediately collapses under the sheer weight of the institutional inventory. We keep losing these trades because we are reacting to the visible momentum on the surface chart, completely blind to the hidden data processing underneath.

Until we learn to read how volume is absorbed in real-time, we will continue to provide the exact liquidity the big players need to fill their accounts.
