The foreign exchange market is the absolute foundation of the global economy. People often think of currency as just another thing to trade, similar to stocks or gold. In institutional finance, it is understood differently. It is Layer 0—the base operating system for all other financial markets.

Without a functioning currency market, the global economy stops completely. It is impossible to buy stocks, sell physical goods, or issue government debt without an underlying currency system. Every international transaction must eventually pass through this base layer. This constant need to process payments creates a massive daily trading volume. Over $7.5 trillion moves through the foreign exchange market every single day. This massive flow is mostly driven by the mechanical need to process global asset purchases, not just by speculative trading.

Understanding this structure changes how to analyze markets. Stocks, government bonds, and commodities are completely dependent on this currency foundation. Their physical value is permanently tied to the currency they are priced in. If a base currency changes its value, the actual global value of the asset immediately changes, regardless of how a specific company performs. Foreign exchange is the primary variable.

Analyzing broader financial markets requires knowing that this currency layer controls the actual value of everything built on top of it.
