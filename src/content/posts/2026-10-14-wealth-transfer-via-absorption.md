A market crash is not a conspiracy. It is a mechanical process of liquidity absorption.

When a macroeconomic shock occurs, public sentiment collapses. Retail participants aggressively execute market sell orders, attempting to exit positions at any cost. This sudden influx of sell-side volume is exactly what primary dealers and large institutions require.

Massive capital cannot enter a position in a quiet market without drastically altering the price. An external catalyst is needed to generate the opposing liquidity for large block orders.

The sharp downward price action on a public chart displays capitulation. However, order flow data reveals the underlying reality: aggressive retail selling is systematically absorbed by institutional limit orders. Large capital does not panic. It mechanically accumulates assets at a mathematical discount, utilizing the public's fear as a pure liquidity mechanism.

Wealth is not evaporating; it is simply undergoing a transfer from those trading on emotion to those trading on market mechanics.
