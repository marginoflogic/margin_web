While decentralized digital markets operate continuously, institutional capital operates on a strict schedule. A common assumption among beginner traders is that trading volume is evenly distributed around the clock. In reality, the traditional financial system concentrates its execution into specific time windows. The most critical of these is the overlap between the London and New York sessions, occurring from 13:00 to 17:00 GMT.

During these four hours, the two largest financial hubs in the world are fully operational at the same time. This convergence creates the deepest liquidity pool available in the global system, processing more than 50% of the entire daily foreign exchange volume. Institutional players, such as Tier-1 banks and massive asset managers, cannot execute billion-dollar orders in a quiet market without causing severe price distortion. They structurally require this exact overlap to absorb their massive order flow efficiently.

This specific window is not just about order volume; it acts as a catalyst for structural volatility. It contains key macroeconomic data releases and mandatory portfolio rebalancing by global funds. The extreme price movements seen during this time are not random retail speculation. They are the direct result of algorithms executing massive capital transfers when the market is at its deepest.

Recognizing this 4-hour window shifts the focus from unstructured, continuous trading to observing where real institutional liquidity enters the system.
