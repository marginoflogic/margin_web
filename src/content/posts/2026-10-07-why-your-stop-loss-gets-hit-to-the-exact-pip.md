When you place a trade, you assume you are participating in the global interbank market. The terrifying reality is that you probably aren't.

The vast majority of retail brokers operate on what is known as the B-Book model. Your orders never leave their servers. The broker acts as your direct counterparty. This creates a catastrophic conflict of interest: your loss is their direct profit.

Because you are trading inside their enclosed system, they have absolute visibility. They see the exact price levels where retail stop-losses and margin calls are clustered. When there is enough money pooled just below a retail support zone, the broker has a massive financial incentive to create a brief price spike to trigger those liquidations.

But the exploitation goes a level higher. Brokers package and sell this aggregated liquidity data to Tier-1 institutions. They don't sell your name; they sell a map of where the retail money is hiding.

When a massive institution needs to execute a billion-dollar order, they look at this map. They push the price exactly into those retail stop-loss clusters. The forced liquidations provide the necessary volume to fill the giant's order without slippage.

This is why the market constantly produces those long wicks that hit your stop-loss to the exact pip before aggressively reversing in your predicted direction. It is not bad luck. It is not a volatile market reacting to news. It is a highly efficient, mathematically driven extraction of retail liquidity.

You are trading with a blindfold, while the house is looking directly at your cards.
