Evaluating the statistical success rate of different trading methodologies requires analyzing how close each method is to raw market data. There is no fixed universal percentage of success for any single approach, as strict risk management and execution determine the final outcome. However, the inherent mathematical edge of each method can be ranked by its data processing hierarchy.

Traditional technical analysis, which relies heavily on mathematical indicators, sits at the bottom of this hierarchy. Indicators are derivative calculations of past price movements. Because they lag behind real-time market mechanics and only react to what has already occurred, relying solely on technical analysis yields a statistically lower probability of success.

Fundamental analysis holds a very high reliability for determining long-term macroeconomic capital flows. Institutional capital moves based on fundamental data, making it highly effective for establishing a broader directional bias. However, on its own, it lacks the micro-structural precision needed for exact trade entry and invalidation.

Price action analysis significantly improves the statistical edge. It removes lagging derivatives and focuses strictly on the structural footprint of the market. It reads the direct result of market mechanics. The highest statistical probability, however, is found in volume analysis. Volume measures the actual capital injected into the market. It identifies exact liquidity pools, accumulation phases, and the presence of institutional activity.

An isolated method rarely survives long-term quantitative testing. The most robust statistical models combine these elements hierarchically.

They use fundamental data for broad context, volume analysis to confirm actual institutional participation, and price action mechanics to execute precise entries and manage risk exposure.
