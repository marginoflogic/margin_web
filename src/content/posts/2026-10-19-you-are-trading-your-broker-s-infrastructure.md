Retail traders consistently misinterpret a widened spread as a malicious attack on their specific position or a sudden shift in the actual market price. When your stop-loss is triggered in a void, you blame "the market." This is a fundamental misunderstanding of the retail ecosystem.

You are not trading directly on the interbank market; you are trading through your broker's technological bridge. When market liquidity dries up during news events or rollover, Tier-1 institutional brokers maintain relatively tight spreads because they have access to a massive pool of global liquidity. Retail brokers, however, operate in a much smaller pond.

When their limited liquidity providers pull their pricing during volatility, the retail broker is suddenly exposed to rejection risk. They do not want to be left holding your directional risk if the market gaps. To protect themselves, their automated algorithms instantly and artificially widen the spread. This acts as a protective shield for their infrastructure, deterring new entries and sweeping up resting retail stop-losses in the process.

If you get swept by a widened spread on a retail platform, you weren't outsmarted by the market.

Your stop-loss was simply the operational fuel required to keep your broker's risk parameters stable. You are trading their technological limitations. Price your risk accordingly.
