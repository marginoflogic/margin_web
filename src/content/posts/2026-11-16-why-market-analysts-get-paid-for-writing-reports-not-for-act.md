There is a major difference between analyzing the market and actually trading it. People often wonder why famous market analysts are not wealthy institutional traders. The answer is based entirely on how they get paid and what their daily job actually requires. Analysts and traders operate in two completely different professions.

The primary job of an analyst is to collect historical data, review economic news, and write structured reports. They receive a steady salary from media companies or financial institutions to produce content. They get paid to talk about the market, not to trade it. Generating wealth in financial markets does not come from simply predicting where a price will go. It comes from putting real money at risk and managing that exposure constantly. Because analysts do not use live capital, their theoretical models do not include the physical reality of trading, such as dealing with low liquidity or managing severe financial losses.

This structural difference shows exactly how market analysis should be used. Using an analyst's report to enter a live trade is a mechanical error, because their reports do not show real-time money moving in the market. However, reading these reports still holds strong value. They are highly efficient for collecting broad economic facts and understanding what the general public expects to happen.

A solid trading system uses these reports to understand the big picture, but relies strictly on real-time volume and structural data to actually execute a trade.
